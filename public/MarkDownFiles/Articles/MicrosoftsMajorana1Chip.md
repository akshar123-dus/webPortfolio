## Why This Matters

Tech giants like Google and IBM have been racing to build the most powerful quantum computer. While they’ve made progress, their systems still struggle with errors and need huge amounts of error correction which makes them hard to scale up.
To solve this problem Microsoft applied a different approach to completely solve this by using topological quantum computing. It could make quantum computers much more stable and easier to scale up if it works.
The Problems with Current Quantum Computers
• Qubits are too fragile – They lose information quickly due to outside interference.
• Too much error correction is needed – A single reliable qubit needs thousands of error-prone qubits.
• Scaling up is difficult – As you add more qubits, errors grow fast, making large systems impractical and inefficient.
Microsoft’s Majorana 1 chip aims to solve these problems and make large-scale quantum computing possible.

## HOW?

Quantum computing has always been an area with great potential but a minefield to navigate. It was hindered by many major challenges one of which, perhaps even the biggest one, is qubit stability. Traditional quantum computers rely on qubits that are prone to errors, requiring massive error correction methods that limit scalability. These error correction methods require expending thousands of physical qubits to get one usable one, making any quantum computer far too complex and hard to build. But, as mentioned above Microsoft has taken a bold and innovative approach to solve this problem: topological qubits, powered by something called Majorana zero mode.
