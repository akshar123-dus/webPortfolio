# The Future of 6G Technology: Beyond Faster Speeds

With 5G right around the corner, many people can't help but anticipate the arrival of its successor, 6G. However, what is 6G, what are its potential applications, and what technological challenges does it face? Well, unlike 6G, the answer to those questions can be found in 2024

# What is 6G?

6G, sixth-generation wireless technology, refers to the next generation of mobile network technologies that build upon and surpass the current meta of 5G. It is often characterized by its ultra-high speed wireless communication, extremely low latency, and the integration of advanced technologies like artificial intelligence (AI), terahertz (THz) frequencies, and intelligent network architecture to provide seamless, energy-efficient, and universal connectivity across diverse environments.

# How fast is it?

The best answer would be to compare 6G with something you might have used previously used such as 4G or 5G. Let us first compare 5G and 4G, then 6G against 5G. Comparatively, 5G is faster than 4G and, in ideal conditions, it offers as much as 20 Gbps, 200 times more than what you would experiences on the 4G network. With these speeds, you'd be able to download Fortnite, which is 30 GB, in just 12 seconds or the whole Harry Potter series in just 13 seconds, considering it is in HD and about 32 GB in size. Now, after all this, let's finally do the comparison between 6G and 5G. The 6G network speed will boast as much as 1 Tbps, which would be about 50 times that of 5G. This would make doing the same tasks of downloading Fortnite and the entire Harry Potter series be done in .24 and .26 seconds respectively.

# What are its potential Applications?

There are multiple applications for 6G networks, for example, low latency, high speeds, increased capacity, sustainability and energy efficiency, universal connectivity, and new uses and applications.

1. _Low Latency_
   Latency is the measure of delay within a system, especially in a network or internet connection. It displays the time it takes for data to travel from one point to another. The lower the latency the quicker the machine or software can react. This is especially useful in activities where quick outputs must be given out. 6G aims to reduce latency to as low as 10-100 microseconds, making it perfect for applications like autonomous vehicles, remote surgery, and real-time gaming where quick outputs must be given out.2. New uses and applications
   6G will allow new use cases and applications, and it will bring many exciting new possibilities. It will allow holographic communication, or HTC, where you can have 3D-like video calls that feel more real, like you're with someone. It will also improve IoT and sensor networks to make smart cities and industries where things like traffic lights and machines talk to each other to make everything run smoothly. Real-time language translation will make it easier to talk to anyone, no matter what language they speak, and augmented reality (AR) will let you interact with digital objects as if they’re part of the real world. Plus, 6G will help keep your data more secure and private, giving you more control over your personal information.

# What technological challenges does it face?

6G will face some challenges, like using terahertz (THz) frequencies for fast data transfer, which don’t travel very far and can be blocked by obstacles. To solve this, the network will need a lot of energy to send signals over long distances, and picking the right semiconductors (materials that control the flow of electricity) is key to making this possible. With artificial intelligence and machine learning being used more, there will be concerns about security and privacy, but tools like federated learning, deep learning, and neural learning can help keep data safe and make the network smarter. Also, with more IoT devices (like smart gadgets) connected, 6G will have to manage the large amount of data storage and processing needed on local devices while making sure the network runs smoothly and delivers high-quality service.

# When can we expect it?

In the words of Ericsson’s chief technology officer (CTO) Erik Ekudden- “It’s a little too early to talk about 6G.” - however, a 2021 report by ABI Research predicted that early commercial deployment will occur in 2028 and 2029, with more widespread rollouts coming in the following years.
